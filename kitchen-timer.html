Decent Timer

Four big timers on one screen.
Readable from across the kitchen.

Two timers count up and two count down, under a large clock. Each running timer shows when it started and when it'll be done. Big buttons for messy hands, a screen that never sleeps, and a full-screen clock when nothing is running. Made for an iPad on the counter top, and it works on an iPhone too.

What it's for

A timer for real cooking

Cooking rarely needs just one timer. Decent Timer keeps four on screen at once, big enough to read without walking over, so the bread, the rice and the sauce each get their own.

Four at once

Two count up, for "how long has this been going?". Two count down, for "tell me when it's done".

Readable from afar

Huge numbers, grey when idle, white when running, orange when a count-down has finished.

Gentle, but hard to miss

When a count-down ends it plays a soft ding and pulses the screen, then shows how long ago it finished.

Nothing to sign up for

No account, no ads, no tracking. It never touches the network.

How it works

Tap, and it's timing

There's nothing to set up. Each timer is one card with one big button.

1

Count up

Tap Up and the card starts counting from zero, showing the time you started. Stop pauses it and Up carries on; Reset puts it back to 0:00.

2

Count down

Choose a time with – and +, one minute a tap. Hold either button and it changes fast, speeding up the longer you hold. Then tap Down. While it runs, the card shows when it started and its ETA, the time it will be done. And + and – still work, to add a few minutes.

3

Or just type it

Tap the time on a count-down that isn't running and a keypad fills the screen. Type a number and pick hours, mins or secs, or type it as you'd say it: 1.5h, 90s, 1:30, or just 5 for five minutes.

When time is up

A gentle ding, then the time past

When a count-down reaches zero, Decent Timer plays a soft ding, not a harsh alarm. Then it keeps counting, below zero and in orange, so it shows the time past: -0:15 means it finished 15 seconds ago. Come back after a phone call and you know exactly how long the pasta has been over.

  • The ding repeats every 5 seconds until you tap Stop or Reset.
  • The whole screen pulses, so you notice it from the other side of the room even with the sound off.

When nothing's running

A clock for the counter

After an hour with no timers running and nobody touching it, Decent Timer switches to a full-screen clock with the day of the week. To get there straight away, tap the clock at the top.

The clock drifts a little every minute so it doesn't burn into the screen. It never takes over while a timer is running. Tap anywhere to get the timers back.

Started at, ready at

Know when it'll be ready

A running timer shows the time you started it, so "how long has the oven been on?" has an answer even if you forgot to look.

A running count-down also shows its ETA: the clock time it will reach zero. "ETA 10:44" is easier to plan around than "4:57 left", and it moves when you add or take off minutes with + and –.

iPad, iPhone and Mac

Fits whatever you've got

  • iPad, either way up. In landscape each card puts its buttons beside the time; in portrait the cards stand taller, with bigger numbers.
  • iPhone, in portrait or landscape, with the start and finish times on each card.
  • Mac, as the same app running natively (Mac Catalyst), if you build it yourself.
  • The original iPad mini from 2012, still on iOS 9. A separate 32-bit build gives an old iPad a job on the kitchen counter (it needs a jailbroken iPad: how to install it).

Install

Get it onto your iPad or iPhone

Decent Timer is installed with SideStep, a free Mac app that puts apps on your own iPad or iPhone with your own Apple ID and keeps them signed. Click the button on a Mac with the iPad or iPhone plugged in. If you don't have SideStep yet, the button offers an installer that sets it up first and walks you through it.

Prefer another sideloader? Download DecentTimer-1.3.ipa and open it in AltStore, SideStore or a similar app.

Have an old iPad on iOS 9? See installing on iOS 9.

Old iPads

Installing on iOS 9

A separate 32-bit build runs on first-generation iPads that are stuck on iOS 9: the iPad mini 1 and the iPad 2, 3 and 4. It was built and tested on an iPad mini 1 on iOS 9.3.5.

The iPad has to be jailbroken, because the app installs as a system app rather than through the App Store or a sideloader. It won't run on a current iPad: use the install button above for those.

Download the iOS 9 build Release notes

Over SSH

Unpack the download on your computer, copy the app to the iPad, then refresh the home screen:

tar xzf KitchenTimer-1.0-ios9-armv7.tar.gz
scp -r KitchenTimer.app root@<ipad-ip>:/Applications/
ssh root@<ipad-ip> 'chmod -R 755 /Applications/KitchenTimer.app &&
  uicache -p /Applications/KitchenTimer.app && killall SpringBoard'

A freshly jailbroken iPad's root password is alpine; change it.

With Filza

Copy the download to the iPad, extract it in Filza, move KitchenTimer.app into /Applications/, set its permissions to 755, and respring. Decent Timer then appears on the home screen.

Decent Timer

A kitchen timer for iPad and iPhone by John Buckman, made at Decent Espresso. Objective-C and UIKit, one source tree that builds for current iPads and iPhones, the Mac, and a 32-bit iPad mini on iOS 9.
